# Official Proposal Rules - Telkom University Faculty of Informatics (2024 baseline)

## Scope and authority

Source baseline: *Panduan Penulisan Proposal Tugas Akhir Berbasis Riset Program Studi Sarjana, Fakultas Informatika, Universitas Telkom*, established through Dean Decree No. KD. 786/AKD11/IF-DEK/2024, dated 19 November 2024.

Apply this file to **proposal** work. Do not assume it is the complete/current rulebook for the final TA report. Newer lecturer/prodi/faculty documents override this baseline.

## Eligibility and TA shape

The guide states that students taking Penulisan Proposal must have passed Sidang Tingkat 2 and passed at least 110 credits, except students approved for accelerated completion by the Head of Study Program.

The Faculty of Informatics recognizes Skripsi, Prototipe, and Proyek among the listed undergraduate TA output types in the cited university rule. The guide focuses on research-oriented TA and expects systematic research methodology in computing/informatics.

## Required proposal package

The proposal is described as a plan for the TA and should communicate the topic, work plan, and readiness. The guide lists:

1. Sampul Muka
2. Lembar Persetujuan
3. Abstrak
4. Pendahuluan
   - 1.1 Latar Belakang
   - 1.2 Perumusan Masalah
   - 1.3 Tujuan
   - 1.4 Hipotesis (opsional)
   - 1.5 Rencana Kegiatan
   - 1.6 Jadwal Kegiatan
5. Kajian Pustaka
6. Perancangan Sistem **atau** Alur Pemodelan
7. Daftar Pustaka
8. Lampiran

## Cover and approval page

The cover example includes: proposal title, Penulisan Proposal class, student identity (NIM and name), Telkom University logo, study program/faculty/university/location, and submission year.

The approval page includes an Indonesian title, English title, student identity, study program/faculty, date, and approval/signatures of prospective supervisor(s).

Do not hardcode the sample course code shown in the 2024 figure. Use the current course/class code supplied by the user or current LMS/RPS because sample codes in older figures can become stale.

## Abstract

The official guide expects the abstract to summarize the planned TA, including:

1. short problem description (about 1-2 sentences);
2. main objective;
3. method/solution offered (about 1-3 sentences);
4. planned data source/type and/or case study;
5. initial hypothesis.

Add keywords describing the proposal content. The guide states a maximum of 6 keywords and specifies lowercase/non-capital form.

Treat the abstract as a summary of a **plan**, not completed research. Do not invent results.

## Chapter 1 - Pendahuluan

### 1.1 Latar Belakang

The guide expects the background to justify why the proposed TA is important using prior research. It should cover the reason the case/problem/phenomenon is studied, whether a new concept is sought, and the gap between the current and expected condition. It also asks the writer to describe how the problem was identified.

The guide states the background is at least 1 page.

Practical interpretation: build the background as an evidence-backed chain from context -> observed problem -> prior work -> limitation/gap -> why the proposed work is necessary.

### 1.2 Perumusan Masalah

Derive the problem formulation from the background and prior research. Definitions, assumptions, scope, and boundaries may be included. The guide explicitly says the problem formulation does **not** have to be a question.

### 1.3 Tujuan

State the TA objective briefly and specifically. The guide allows objectives such as explaining, proving, applying a concept/phenomenon/hypothesis, or building a model, and says the objective must be clear and measurable.

### 1.4 Hipotesis (optional)

The hypothesis is a provisional answer to the problem. The guide describes it as including the method/concept to solve the problem, the reason for selecting it, differences from previous methods/concepts, and an expected outcome from the planned solution.

Do not force a hypothesis where the research design does not naturally require one; the guide marks this section optional.

### 1.5 Rencana Kegiatan

Include planned steps for:

- literature study;
- data collection (qualitative/quantitative as applicable);
- research design, including research procedure and system design;
- testing/evaluation;
- interpretation and conclusion process.

### 1.6 Jadwal Kegiatan

Build the schedule from the actual problem-solving methodology. The guide allows a bar chart by week or month.

## Chapter 2 - Kajian Pustaka

Start with a synthesis of papers/journals related to the research question or problem statement. Supporting theory is optional and may come from textbooks, papers/journals, theses, and prior TAs, with proper citation and academic integrity.

Do not treat this as a glossary chapter. Its primary purpose is to show what is known, compare approaches, identify limitations, and support the research gap and method choice.

## Chapter 3 - Perancangan Sistem or Alur Pemodelan

Choose the title that matches the work.

### Perancangan Sistem

Describe the system design using a block/process diagram or flowchart and explanation. It can include data requirements, data collection/preprocessing, and the planned testing scenario.

### Alur Pemodelan

Describe the model-building flow using a block diagram or flowchart and explanation. Include stages based on the selected method and the testing/evaluation scenario.

## References

The proposal-specific section says there should be at least **10 references**, all listed references must be cited in the document, and sources used to establish the research gap/opportunity should come from publications within the last **5 years**.

The guide uses numbered citations ordered by appearance with Arabic numerals in square brackets.

### Important internal inconsistency

A separate comparison table for S1/S2/S3 in the same guide says S1 has a minimum of **20** references. This conflicts with the proposal-specific section that says minimum **10**.

Do not silently resolve this conflict. Prefer a newer/current lecturer or prodi instruction. If no clarification is available, explicitly flag the ambiguity. A conservative practical target of 20+ may reduce risk, but label that as a precautionary recommendation, **not** as a verified official minimum for the proposal.

## Submission medium and typography

The guide allows hardcopy or PDF softcopy, depending on lecturer/team instruction.

It specifies:

- paper: A4, 80 gsm for hardcopy;
- font: Times New Roman;
- chapter title: 16 pt;
- subchapter title: 14 pt;
- sub-subchapter and below: 12 pt;
- body: 12 pt;
- body line spacing: 1.5;
- left margin: 4 cm.

### Important margin typo/ambiguity

The guide then says the “top, left, and bottom” margins are 3 cm, which conflicts with the immediately stated left margin of 4 cm. This is likely a wording error (commonly intended as top/right/bottom 3 cm), but the skill must **not** present that correction as verified fact. Check the current template or lecturer instruction before final formatting.

## Language/style rules

The guide requires formal Indonesian, standard terminology, concise and clear writing, and disciplinary writing conventions. It states that first-person pronouns such as *saya* and *kami* should not be used except inside quotations. Sentences should be complete and punctuation used properly.

## Process-related official facts useful for writing

The guide states:

- minimum proposal guidance/bimbingan: 3 times;
- Desk Evaluation uses Form TA1-02;
- proposal presentation uses Form TA1-03;
- passing the course requires a final grade above 50 according to the guide;
- late submission to the second DE stage can cap the DE score at 51 under the described condition.
