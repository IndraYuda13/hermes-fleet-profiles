# Proposal Process and Working Cadence

## Official sequence in the bundled 2024 guide

The guide shows this high-level sequence:

1. proposal-writing material;
2. explanation/offering of TA topics;
3. proposal guidance;
4. Desk Evaluation;
5. proposal presentation;
6. course graduation decision;
7. supervisor determination and TA decree issuance.

## Official semester timeline baseline

The guide describes approximately:

- Weeks 1-3: proposal-writing material; EPrT test if needed.
- Weeks 4-7: TA topic presentation, topic selection, discussion with prospective supervisors.
- Week 8: proposal-writing progress and EPrT certificate collection.
- Weeks 9-10: proposal guidance.
- Weeks 11-12: submit proposal for Desk Evaluation.
- Week 13: revise from DE.
- Week 14: proposal seminar.
- Week 15: course graduation decision.
- Weeks 17-18: grade upload.
- Weeks 18-20: student uploads signed proposal.
- Weeks 21-22: administrative verification.
- Weeks 22-23: TA decree issuance.

A later table in the same guide gives a more detailed SK TA flow around weeks 15-19. Treat exact week numbering as a baseline and follow the current class schedule if it differs.

## Guidance requirement

The bundled guide requires at least **3 guidance sessions** during proposal preparation.

Use each session as a decision checkpoint, not a status report. Before a session, prepare:

- changes since last session;
- current research logic in one paragraph;
- 2-5 concrete questions/decisions needed;
- new literature/evidence added;
- unresolved risks;
- next milestone.

After a session, convert feedback into a revision ledger:

| Feedback | Interpretation | Planned change | Evidence needed | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Do not rewrite blindly if feedback is ambiguous; isolate the decision that needs clarification.

## Recommended working cadence

### Stage A - Problem framing

Deliverables:

- 1-paragraph problem context;
- stakeholder/system/context scope;
- list of evidence required;
- 5-15 candidate references;
- initial problem statement.

Exit condition: the problem is specific, evidence-backed, and feasible.

### Stage B - Literature and gap

Deliverables:

- evidence matrix;
- thematic synthesis;
- comparison of methods/approaches;
- explicit limitations in prior work;
- candidate research gap.

Exit condition: the gap follows from sources rather than from unsupported claims.

### Stage C - Research design

Deliverables:

- objective(s);
- method rationale;
- data/source plan;
- system/model flow;
- evaluation scenario and metric(s);
- expected contribution framed conservatively.

Exit condition: `problem -> objective -> method -> evaluation` is one coherent chain.

### Stage D - Proposal assembly

Deliverables:

- Chapter 1 complete;
- Chapter 2 synthesis complete;
- Chapter 3 design/model flow complete;
- schedule;
- abstract drafted last;
- references and citations cross-checked.

### Stage E - DE hardening

Audit against [rubrics.md](rubrics.md). Focus first on highest-weight weak components.

Create a “reviewer attack list”:

- weakest evidence claim;
- unclear scope/boundary;
- biggest feasibility risk;
- least justified method choice;
- weakest evaluation metric;
- most likely novelty/contribution challenge.

Resolve or explicitly delimit each one.

### Stage F - Seminar preparation

Prepare:

- a 1-sentence problem;
- a 1-sentence gap;
- a 1-sentence objective;
- a 1-sentence method;
- a 1-sentence evaluation plan;
- one visual research flow;
- one literature comparison visual/table;
- concise answers to likely questions.

## Revision protocol

When receiving feedback:

1. classify each comment as rule/compliance, evidence, logic, method, scope, clarity, or formatting;
2. identify the exact artifact/paragraph affected;
3. determine whether new evidence is required;
4. revise the smallest necessary scope first;
5. rerun alignment and rubric checks;
6. keep a decision log for changes that alter the research design.

Do not “polish” away a substantive unresolved issue.
