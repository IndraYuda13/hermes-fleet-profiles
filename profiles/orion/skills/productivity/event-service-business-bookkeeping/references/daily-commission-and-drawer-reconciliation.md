# Daily Commission & Cash Drawer Reconciliation Architecture

Standard operating procedures, mathematical models, and field reconciliation controls for high-turnover walk-in service businesses (barbershops, beauty/nail salons, massage/reflexology, auto-detailing).

---

## 1. Daily Multi-Staff Commission Model

Unlike milestone-based event businesses, walk-in service businesses settle commissions on a **daily closing cadence**.

### Mathematical Formulation per Staff Member (i)
1. **Gross Revenue per Staff (R_i):**
   Total revenue generated by services performed by Staff i.
2. **Gross Commission (C_i):**
   $$C_i = R_i \times P_i$$
   *(where P_i is staff i's negotiated commission rate, ranging from 10% to 70%; e.g. 10%–25% for junior/trainee, 30%–50% for standard capster, and 55%–70% for senior master barber or revenue-split chair rentals)*
3. **Shop Share from Staff (S_i):**
   $$S_i = R_i \times (1 - P_i) = R_i - C_i$$
4. **Net Take-Home Pay (THP_i):**
   $$THP_i = \max(0, C_i - K_i + T_i)$$
   *(where K_i = daily cash advance / kasbon makan; T_i = customer tip received, 100% staff entitlement)*

### Shop Owner Net Daily Inflow (S_net)
$$S_{\text{net}} = \left(\sum_{i=1}^N S_i\right) - E_{\text{shop}}$$
*(where E_shop = petty cash operational expenses paid from the register, such as disposable razor blades, neck strips, sanitizers, or client refreshments)*

## 2. The Cash Drawer vs QRIS/Transfer Discrepancy (Crucial Field Failure Mechanism)

### The Problem
In modern retail, 50%–80% of customer payments are completed via **QRIS or Bank Transfer**, which settle directly and immediately into the **owner's bank account**.
However, capsters, stylists, and therapists expect their **daily commission paid in physical cash** from the shop register drawer at closing time.

### Cash Drawer Reconciliation Invariant
At daily closing, compare physical cash collected (Cash_drawer) against total cash required to disburse staff take-home pay and petty cash expenses:
$$\Delta_{\text{drawer}} = Cash_{\text{drawer}} - \left(\sum_{i=1}^N THP_i + E_{\text{shop}}\right)$$

#### Case A: Cash Surplus (Delta_drawer >= 0)
- **Action:**
  1. Cashier disburses THP_i in cash to each staff member.
  2. Cashier reimburses register for approved petty expenses E_shop.
  3. Remaining physical cash balance Delta_drawer is bagged and deposited/transferred to the business bank account as physical store earnings.

#### Case B: Cash Deficit (Delta_drawer < 0)
- **Mechanism:** Customers paid heavily via QRIS/EDC, leaving insufficient physical banknotes in the register to cover staff daily commissions.
- **Action (Fail-Closed Protocol):**
  1. Cashier disburses all remaining physical drawer cash (Cash_drawer - E_shop) proportionally or up to available liquidity.
  2. The deficit amount |Delta_drawer| must be **transferred by the owner via mobile banking directly to the capsters' bank/e-wallet accounts** that same evening.
  3. Under no circumstances should the register float / modal awal be used without documentation.

## 4. Lightweight Client-Side Mobile Tool Pattern

For barbershops and salons where staff lack laptops or complex POS software:
- Implement a responsive, single-file HTML/JS web utility deployed on cPanel / static hosting.
- Use `localStorage` to persist staff names and negotiated commission rates so they do not need re-entry every morning.
- Include custom percentage selection across 10% to 70% (via quick-pill selectors or range slider) to accommodate both junior assistants (10%–25%), standard capsters (30%–50%), and senior partners (55%–70%).
- Include auto-formatting currency inputs (`id-ID` locale with dots) and instant 1-click clipboard copying using `navigator.clipboard.writeText()`.
- Provide visual color-coding for Capster 1 (Cyan), Capster 2 (Purple), and Shop Share (Emerald) to maintain clarity during hectic closing shifts.

## 5. Micro-Enterprise Weekly Cashflow Modeling (Warkop & Small F&B Stalls)

For high-turnover micro food stalls and independent warkop/warmindo operations:

### A. The 4-Week Cashflow Staging Model
1. **Week 0 (Setup & Capex Invariant):**
   - Outlay capital allocated to 3 distinct buckets: Equipment buyout (Capex ~40%–45%), Month-1 premises rent (inclusive of utilities, ~14%), and initial fast-moving inventory + register float (~25%).
   - Strict 10% reserve buffer kept as liquid cash cushion.
2. **Week 1 (Introduction & Opening Traffic):**
   - Baseline customer count (~20–25 orders/day). Positive net operational cashflow begins immediately due to low overhead.
3. **Week 2 (Growth & Fast Payback Point / BEP):**
   - As repeat foot traffic establishes (~35 orders/day), cumulative operational net inflows reach ~100% of initial startup capital. Standard payback period is achieved within 14 operational days.
4. **Week 3 (Stabilized Run-Rate):**
   - Regular customer base (commuters, delivery riders, students) locks in ~40–50 orders/day.
5. **Week 4 (Monthly Closing & Rent Reservation):**
   - Mandatory operational reserve: before calculating net monthly take-home profit, immediately carve out and set aside the full rent for Month 2 (Rp 350.000–Rp 500.000) into an untouchable envelope.

### B. Daily Cash Register SOP (The 2-Pocket System)
- Every evening during register closing, physical cash receipts must be partitioned 50:50:
  - **Pocket 1 (50% Restock Wallet):** Reserved exclusively for tomorrow morning's ingredient replenishment (instant noodles, eggs, ice, ground coffee).
  - **Pocket 2 (50% Gross Margin Wallet):** Deposited into the owner's bank account or secure cash box.
- **Consignment Snacks (Zero-Capex Inventory):**
  - Partner with local community residents to stock fried snacks (gorengan) and traditional pastries on a 100% consignment basis.
  - The stall earns Rp 300 – Rp 500 net margin per piece with Rp 0 capex and zero unsold-inventory risk.

### C. Chat Response & Reporting Presentation Standard
- **No LaTeX Math in Chat:** Never use LaTeX formatting (`$`, `\rightarrow`, `\text{...}`) in chat messages. Use plain text or standard arrows (`->`, `👉`).
- **No Markdown Tables in Chat Prose:** Present financial and operational breakdowns using clear labeled lines or bullet points.
- **Single-Page Executive PDF Delivery:** If generating PDF reports, enforce exact 1-page A4 geometry, suppress Chrome print artifacts via `--no-pdf-header-footer`, and omit signature/stamp blocks unless explicitly requested.

## 6. Retrospective Monthly Commission Auditing & 2-Page Executive PDF Standard

When an owner requests a retrospective financial recalculation across past monthly data (e.g., shifting historical daily logs from arbitrary day-rates to a fixed commission model such as 60% per head / gross revenue):

### A. Mathematical Retrospective Invariants
1. **Daily Recalculation Loop:**
   For every operational day $t \in [1..D]$:
   - Daily Gross Omzet: $R_t = \text{Cash}_t + \text{QRIS}_t$
   - Daily Staff Commission: $C_t = \text{round}(R_t \times P)$ (where $P$ is the agreed rate, e.g. 60%)
   - Daily Shop Gross Share: $S_t = R_t - C_t = R_t \times (1 - P)$
   - Daily Shop Net Earnings: $N_t = S_t - E_t$ (where $E_t$ is daily store opex: utilities, supplies, maintenance)
2. **Monthly Aggregate Reconciliation:**
   - $\sum R_t = \sum \text{Cash}_t + \sum \text{QRIS}_t$
   - $\sum C_t = P \times \sum R_t$
   - $\sum N_t = (1 - P) \times \sum R_t - \sum E_t$
3. **QRIS Bank vs Cash Drawer Cross-Settlement:**
   - Total cash in register is often less than total staff commission ($\sum \text{Cash}_t < \sum C_t$).
   - Reconcile the exact amount to transfer from the bank's QRIS settlement account to the capsters:
     $$\text{Transfer}_{\text{bank}\to\text{capster}} = \sum C_t - \text{CashPaid}_{\text{drawer}}$$
   - Remaining funds in the bank account represent the owner's net cash profit.

### B. 2-Page Executive PDF Layout Architecture
When rendering monthly financial statements to PDF via headless Chrome:
- **Halaman 1: Executive Financial Statement & P&L:**
  - 4 Top KPI Cards: Gross Omzet, Total Capster Commission (with take-home note), Operating Expenses, and Net Shop Profit (with profit margin %).
  - Formal Multi-Tier Income Statement (Revenue breakdown by payment channel, Operating expenses categorized by utility/consumables/fees, and Net Operating Profit).
  - Side-by-side Liquidity Reconciliation (Physical cash drawer vs QRIS bank account).
- **Halaman 2: Daily Audit Journal (Lampiran Jurnal Harian):**
  - Complete daily table covering all calendar days (e.g. 1–31 August).
  - Closed/off days explicitly marked (`TUTUP OPERASIONAL`) with visual distinction.
  - Column styling: Commission column highlighted in bold blue; Daily Net Shop Share colored green for surplus (`+Rp X`) and red for operational deficit (`-Rp Y`).
  - Total accumulation row at the bottom strictly identical to Page 1 figures.
- **Visual & Formatting Rules:**
  - **Zero Signatures / Stamps:** Strictly omit signature blocks, signee lines ("Mengetahui / Dibuat Oleh"), and stamp graphics (user preference: modern digital paperless format).
  - Footer must contain digital system notice: `"Dicetak secara digital oleh Sistem Informasi Manajemen Keuangan Barbershop • Halaman X dari Y"`.
  - Compile HTML with zero page overflow, print using `google-chrome-stable --headless --disable-gpu --no-sandbox --no-pdf-header-footer --print-to-pdf=<output.pdf> <input.html>`, and verify via PyMuPDF rendering before delivery.
